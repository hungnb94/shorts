# MONEY BLINDSPOT Long-Form Strategy — 90-Day Pilot

Date: 2026-07-28
Status: Active strategy; first flagship completed locally, not published

## Decision

Make 8–12 minute evidence-led business explainers the primary authority asset. Use Shorts only as derivative discovery once a flagship is published; do not continue building unrelated standalone Shorts with no destination.

This strategy does not assume that long-form automatically wins. It chooses long-form because it better supports trust, watch time, sponsorship, qualified leads and an evergreen library while preserving the current research, TTS, data-visualization and ffmpeg advantages.

## Positioning

MONEY BLINDSPOT audits the number, contract or infrastructure constraint hidden inside a popular business story.

Viewer contract:

> We do not repeat the viral claim. We show the mechanism, the evidence, who benefits, who bears the risk and the questions you can reuse on the next claim.

Avoid:

- Generic billionaire motivation.
- Stock-pick lists.
- Raw interview compilations.
- “AI will change everything” commentary without a causal mechanism.
- Current news with no evergreen business lesson.

## Channel Choice

Use the existing MONEY BLINDSPOT channel rather than creating another empty channel. Its current subscriber base is too small to create meaningful long-form audience baggage, while the existing name and business archive fit the new promise. Keep prior Shorts; do not delete history to manufacture a reset. Separate flagships and derivative Shorts with playlists and Related Video wiring.

Expect the first long-form upload to have little native distribution. Do not interpret low views from insufficient impressions as proof that the format failed. Earn qualified initial traffic through derivative Shorts, relevant professional communities and direct sharing to people who genuinely follow AI infrastructure; never use click farms or unrelated traffic.

## Topic Portfolio

Every topic must combine two demand pools:

1. A current attention wave.
2. An evergreen human/business question.

Examples:

| Current wave | Evergreen question | Money Blindspot wedge |
|---|---|---|
| AI data-center construction | What is the real bottleneck in a gold rush? | Deliverable megawatts, transformers and grid contracts |
| Stablecoin adoption | Who controls payment rails and earns the spread? | The business model behind digital dollars |
| Founder success stories | When should you ignore consensus? | Survivable bets versus blind confidence |
| Small-business acquisition | Does revenue equal owner wealth? | Price, cash flow, labor and capital at risk |
| Private-credit expansion | Who owns the risk when banks move it elsewhere? | Yield, liquidity and loss allocation |

## Topic Gate

Score each candidate before deep production:

- Current demand: 25 points.
- Evergreen demand: 20 points.
- Direct competition gap: 20 points.
- Evidence availability: 15 points.
- Visual feasibility with licensed/original assets: 10 points.
- Monetization/audience value: 10 points.

Do not greenlight below 75/100. A low-competition topic with no audience is not an opportunity. A large topic with identical packaging from established channels is not an opportunity either.

## Format Template

1. Contrarian object or number in the first 15 seconds.
2. Proof-backed promise before 30 seconds.
3. Current demand curve.
4. Causal mechanism.
5. Revealed-preference evidence: contracts, capital expenditure or behavior.
6. Who benefits.
7. Counterargument and second-order risk.
8. Reusable audit framework.
9. Loop closure that answers the opening question.
10. One compact comment/subscribe CTA.

The template is a quality floor, not a script formula. Each episode still needs a distinct Signature Moment and evidence object.

## Packaging Strategy

Generate three competing title/thumbnail packages before deep editing:

- Curiosity package.
- Specific consequence package.
- Contrarian mechanism package.

The title and thumbnail must contribute different information. Do not repeat the same phrase in both.

For the first flagship:

- Title: `AI's Real Bottleneck Isn't Chips`
- Thumbnail: `4-YEAR WAIT`
- Promise: the title creates the gap; the thumbnail supplies the consequence.

## Production System

Use the current strengths:

- Official-source web research.
- Evidence ledger before scripting.
- Canonical Qwen Finance narrator.
- 4K Pexels illustration with explicit manifest and semantic labels.
- Original charts/diagrams rather than copied screenshots.
- Python + ffmpeg renderer.
- Final ASR, full decode, black/freeze/silence/loudness checks.
- Dense hook/full/tail contact-sheet review.

New long-form requirements:

- 1920×1080, 30fps, H.264/AAC.
- 8–12 minutes unless research proves another duration.
- Original narration is the editorial spine.
- No third-party YouTube footage unless rights and transformative purpose are reviewed separately.
- Full English SRT.
- A canonical 1280×720 thumbnail under 2 MB.
- Exact chapters and visible primary-source links.

## Operating Cadence

Capacity hypothesis: one flagship every 10–14 days, but quality gates override the calendar. Do not publish a weak episode only to preserve cadence.

For each published flagship:

1. Publish the long-form authority asset.
2. Create four to six derivative Shorts from different hook mechanisms.
3. Point every derivative to the long-form via Related Video.
4. Avoid uploading all derivatives simultaneously; stagger based on channel eligibility and distribution state.
5. Record package, source, traffic source and retention decisions.

## Measurement Gates

Wait at least 48 hours before drawing conclusions. Treat these as directional experiment gates, not universal YouTube benchmarks:

- Fewer than 1,000 qualified impressions: insufficient evidence for a packaging verdict.
- CTR below roughly 4% after sufficient Browse/Suggested impressions: test package B before blaming the topic.
- First-30-second retention below roughly 65%: opening promise or pace is weak.
- Average percentage viewed below roughly 35%: mechanism/body structure likely needs compression.
- Strong CTR but weak early retention: title/thumbnail overpromised or the proof arrived too late.
- Weak CTR but strong retention: content works for clickers; packaging is the primary problem.
- Strong views but no comments, subscribers or qualified actions: broad curiosity did not transfer into authority.

Do not modify the completed exact-final video after handoff based on early noise. Apply lessons to the next material revision or next episode.

## Failure Loop

Every underperforming episode triggers:

1. Extract the exact failure: topic, package, first 30 seconds, body progression, proof, CTA or distribution.
2. Research three current domain experts or winning case studies that solved the same failure.
3. Change one dominant variable, not every variable.
4. Produce the next test.
5. Log the result and update the format registry.

Never stop at “the algorithm did not push it.” That is not an actionable diagnosis.

## Monetization Architecture

Priority order:

1. High-value evidence-led content service for founders/B2B companies.
2. Relevant B2B sponsors: market intelligence, cloud-cost tools, energy analytics and professional research tools.
3. Owned audience after a real newsletter/landing page exists.
4. Carefully matched affiliate offers.
5. AdSense as a secondary output, not the business model.

Do not insert a fake lead magnet or dead sales link. The V1 CTA remains comment + subscribe until a real destination is built.

## Game-Theory Moat

Large channels can outspend MONEY BLINDSPOT on footage and celebrity access. Competing on spectacle alone is losing.

The defensible wedge is:

- Faster official-source research.
- Visible caveats competitors omit.
- Original causal diagrams.
- A reusable audit framework in every episode.
- A production system that can turn the same research into a flagship, derivative Shorts and sales proof.

The audience should eventually recognize the method before it recognizes the topic.

## 90-Day Outcomes

The objective is not a fixed upload count. The objective is enough validated evidence to answer:

1. Can this positioning win qualified clicks?
2. Can the first 30 seconds retain viewers without sensational claims?
3. Which topic family creates the best combination of CTR, watch time and authority transfer?
4. Can one flagship produce useful derivative Shorts without becoming repetitive?
5. Can the content generate conversations with potential clients or sponsors before meaningful AdSense exists?

Keep the strategy only if real data supports it. Preserve the evidence-led method even if the first title, topic family or channel package must change.
