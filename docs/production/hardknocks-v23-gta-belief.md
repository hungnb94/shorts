## The Common Denominator

The common denominator is not “show a rich person” or “put a big number on screen.” It is a five-stage information contract:

### 1. Frame-zero recognizability

A real human is already speaking and moving. Captions are present immediately. There is no logo intro, establishing shot, freeze, generic B-roll or narrator setup.

### 2. One-sentence conflict

The opening is understandable with no biography:

- V18: most smart friends predicted complete failure;
- V21: lose everything tomorrow and rebuild it all.

Both contain a hard contrast and an extreme boundary.

### 3. Specificity before explanation

The viewer gets a number or absolute condition before context:

- `more than two-thirds`;
- `lost everything tomorrow`.

Specificity makes the claim feel testable rather than inspirational.

### 4. Micro-payoff by about three seconds

The first beat pays off quickly:

- the prediction was `complete failure`;
- the answer is `absolutely`.

The video does not force the viewer to wait ten seconds for the first meaningful answer.

### 5. The payoff opens a larger loop

The micro-payoff immediately creates a second-order question:

- what did Hoffman know that his smart friends did not?;
- what would the businessman buy, and why would that rebuild wealth?

This is **question → answer → bigger question**, not one vague question stretched across the whole Short.

## What Must Not Be Copied Blindly

- A money number without conflict is not the pattern.
- A caption saying `WAIT FOR IT` is not a curiosity gap.
- Fast cuts without information progression are decorative noise.
- A narrator claiming authority is weaker than the subject demonstrating it in the source.
- Early metrics at this sample size do not justify converting every future video to this exact topic or visual treatment.
- The reusable unit is the information architecture, not LinkedIn, buying businesses, or wealthy male interview subjects.
