## Executive decision

Produce a new diagnostic Short from School of Hard Knocks source `DwrRvp_qsRk`, but do not repeat its generic “real-life Monopoly” framing. The selected editorial wedge is a counter-audit of one specific leverage claim:

> If a buyer puts `$2M` down on a `$10M` property and a bank supplies `$8M`, who absorbs a property-value loss first?

The answer is the transferable MONEY BLINDSPOT: in a simplified balance-sheet example, a 20% fall in property value reduces a `$10M` asset to `$8M`; against an unchanged `$8M` loan balance, the buyer's `$2M` first-loss equity is reduced to zero. This does not mean the bank has no risk. Cash flow, amortization, refinancing terms, guarantees, recourse, collateral recovery and transaction costs determine the real outcome.

This is not a “debt makes you rich” edit. It is a visual risk waterfall: source claim → ownership split → value shock → equity wipeout → contract/cash-flow guardrail.
