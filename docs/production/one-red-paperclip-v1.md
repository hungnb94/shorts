## Core Promise

> Can this paperclip buy that house?

The Short follows one physical object through 14 visible trades to one physical payoff. It applies a MrBeast-style challenge engine—simple binary objective, visible escalation, and literal proof—without copying MrBeast's wording, footage, branding, or exact execution.

## MrBeast Principles Applied

Primary references are recorded in `docs/research/one-red-paperclip-2026-07-26/REPORT.md`.

- **Idea before production:** choose a concept understandable in one sentence.
- **Frame-zero promise match:** paperclip and house are visible immediately.
- **One question:** paperclip → house, not multiple competing verdicts.
- **Escalation:** every 2–3 seconds moves to a larger or stranger trade.
- **Proof:** CBC source frames, Kyle identity, actual generator, and actual house payoff.
- **No exposition tax:** viewer does not need legal or historical background.
- **CTA on action:** Like/Subscribe/Comment runs over a moving handshake, not a static interruption card.
- **Literal payoff:** the house appears and stays on screen before the recap.
- **Loop closure:** ending returns to the paperclip and house.

## Million-View Strategy

One million views is a strategic target, not a promised outcome.

### Step 1 — Clear publication gates

- Obtain/record voice-clone consent or replace it with a cleared narrator.
- Clear CBC/TEDx excerpts or replace them with assets that can be published.
- Pass the human Hook Gate.
- Complete a full subjective listening/viewing pass.

Kill criterion: do not upload if any of these remains blocked.

### Step 2 — Lock packaging to the frame-zero promise

- Title, frame-zero visual and first spoken line must all communicate paperclip → house.
- Select a Shorts thumbnail frame that visibly contains both the paperclip and house.
- Do not use Kyle's name as the main cold-viewer anchor.

### Step 3 — Publish into the eligible finance lane

- Confirm destination-channel round-robin and plateau eligibility.
- Use one upload only; do not publish exact duplicate variants simultaneously.
- Record exact upload timestamp, title, thumbnail frame and Studio settings.

### Step 4 — Measure distribution before diagnosing creativity

At 24h and 48h, capture:

- Shown in feed;
- Stayed to watch / Swiped away;
- views;
- average view duration;
- average percentage viewed;
- retention at 1s, 3s, 10s, 30s, 46s and end;
- shares, comments, likes and subscribers gained;
- traffic-source split.

Do not diagnose hook/retention from public views alone.

### Step 5 — Use project decision thresholds

These are internal iteration thresholds, not universal YouTube guarantees:

- `<500` shown in feed at 24h: distribution evidence is inconclusive; keep the upload and wait through the pickup window.
- `>=1,000` shown in feed plus Stayed to watch `<70%`: revise frame-zero/hook.
- Stayed to watch `>=75%` but AVD `<40s`: retain hook; tighten middle escalation.
- AVD `>=50s` but weak shares/comments: strengthen payoff meaning and viewer identity.
- Strong hook/body but low feed share: inspect lane/channel state rather than rebuilding the same creative blindly.

### Step 6 — Map analytics to the EDL

- 0–2.7s drop: hook promise/frame-zero.
- 2.7–12.4s drop: setup or early trade proof.
- 12.4–29.8s drop: escalation density/repetition.
- 34.7–40.2s drop: CTA integration.
- 40.2–52.6s drop: suspense/payoff delay.
- 52.6–61.5s drop: recap/loop value.

Change only the weakest diagnosed axis so the next version remains interpretable.

### Step 7 — Build a portfolio, not one lottery ticket

Repeat the visible-challenge engine across three distinct finance/business topics. Keep the format invariant—physical object, measurable goal, escalating proof, literal payoff—while changing topic. Compare ideas at equal production quality. One video cannot validate or invalidate the entire format.
