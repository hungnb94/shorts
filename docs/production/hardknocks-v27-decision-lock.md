## Why This Strategy Can Win

- **Game theory:** a viewer who commits to BUY/PASS has a reason to wait for the verdict and compare it with their decision.
- **Second-order effect:** the reveal becomes personally diagnostic instead of another passive wealth fact.
- **Third-order risk:** too much UI could increase cognitive load, so the source, audio, captions and body remain frozen and mobile OCR is checked separately.
- **Blue-ocean angle:** the feed interaction is an investment decision, not a generic “how he got rich” claim.
- **Kaizen boundary:** V27 changes one layer only; failure must produce one hook-level lesson rather than a wholesale format reset.

## Post-Production Retro

### What worked

- Three real rough hooks were rendered instead of selecting from copy alone.
- The selected treatment adds personal commitment while preserving the source-native stakes.
- The `77M` counter no longer appears before the source reaches the reveal clause.
- Audio identity allowed stream-scoped QC reuse without pretending the container bytes were unchanged.

### Risks and next improvement

- The countdown UI is denser than V26 and may reduce clarity for low-literacy viewers; phone-scale retention data must decide, not editorial confidence.
- The score difference between forced prediction and identity challenge is only one point; one result cannot establish a universal winner.
- If V27 fails the early-retention hypothesis, the next controlled arm should use `identity_challenge` while freezing the same body and metadata.
