# OUTWISHED — Series Bible

Status: concept backbone; not an approved fourth vertical, production experiment, renderer, or upload authorization.

Working title: **OUTWISHED**  
Genre: high-IQ fantasy comedy / visual slapstick / fair-play puzzle  
Episode target: 50–75 seconds; default writing target 55–65 seconds  
Tagline: **Two minds. One wish. Someone gets played.**

## 1. One-sentence concept

A strategic lamp owner and an improvisational trickster genie hide traps inside the wording and fulfillment of one magical wish, while viewers use visible clues to predict who is really outsmarting whom.

## 2. Audience promise

Every episode promises four simultaneous payoffs:

1. **Prediction** — decide whether the Owner or Genie has the deeper plan.
2. **Puzzle** — spot the decisive clue before the reveal.
3. **Slapstick** — watch a logically earned magical consequence escalate physically.
4. **Rewatch** — confirm that the winning setup was visible before the reversal.

The micro-game is not merely "How will the Genie ruin the wish?" It is:

> **Who planted the real trap?**

This keeps the outcome uncertain after the Owner learns that the Genie deliberately twists wishes.

## 3. Core formula

```text
UNIVERSAL DESIRE
→ HIDDEN PREPARATION
→ BAIT WISH
→ WISH LOCK
→ GENIE EXPLOIT
→ FALSE VICTORY
→ OWNER COUNTER-TRAP
→ OPTIONAL COUNTER-COUNTER
→ EARNED WINNER
→ IRONIC CALLBACK
```

Compact formula:

```text
Wish → Trap → Counter-trap → Reversal
```

Content formula:

```text
Universal desire
× exact wording
× fair visible clue
× escalating fulfillment
× strategic reversal
× ironic consequence
```

## 4. What makes the series distinctive

The familiar "wish gone wrong" trope is only the entry point. The defensible creative engine is a **fair-play duel between two competent opponents**.

- The Owner is not a permanently naive victim.
- The Genie is not an omnipotent author substitute who can invent rules after the fact.
- The decisive move is planted before the reveal.
- Both characters remember prior episodes and adapt.
- Either character can win, and a technical victory can still produce a painful consequence.
- The audience receives the same usable clues as the characters.

The series should feel like a visual logic game disguised as magical slapstick.

## 5. The world contract

### Rule 1 — Exact Words

The Genie must satisfy the exact spoken wording. Implied intent has no authority unless the Owner states it.

### Rule 2 — Minimum Magic

The Genie may change only what is reasonably necessary to satisfy the literal wish. He cannot destroy the world to fulfill a trivial request.

### Rule 3 — No New Conditions

After the Lamp declares `WISH LOCKED`, neither character may add, remove, or reinterpret a clause by inventing a new rule.

### Rule 4 — Preparation Is Legal

Before speaking the wish, the Owner may prepare objects, positions, contracts, witnesses, timers, or environmental conditions. Hidden preparation is the Owner's main strategic weapon.

### Rule 5 — One Execution

Once fulfillment begins, the Genie cannot revise his chosen implementation. A bad choice can trap him.

### Rule 6 — One Wish per Episode

A second wish cannot instantly undo the first. The Owner must solve the consequence through planning, physical action, or the original wish's logic.

### Rule 7 — Visible Clue

The decisive fact must appear before the reveal. It may be subtle, but it cannot be introduced afterward.

### Rule 8 — Reversible Slapstick

Consequences may be painful, humiliating, expensive-looking, or chaotic, but remain stylized, reversible, and non-gory to preserve broad accessibility.

### Modern-world grounding

The rivalry takes place in a recognizable present-day urban world, not a distant fairy-tale kingdom or a full cyberpunk future.

- ordinary problems come from rent, traffic, delivery apps, subscriptions, social media, contactless payments, dating, work, and smart devices;
- Nico combines a smartphone and smartwatch with his physical loophole notebook;
- Veyr visualizes wish clauses as floating app-like contract, timer, payment, and location panels;
- the ancient Lamp has a subtle modern status light while remaining visibly magical;
- modern systems create new loopholes, but interfaces never replace physical action, facial performance, or slapstick;
- technology vocabulary stays simple enough that the conflict remains globally understandable.

## 6. The Lamp as silent referee

The Lamp is a rule interface, not a third protagonist.

Functions:

- displays the exact wish;
- declares `WISH LOCKED`;
- validates whether a fulfillment obeys the contract;
- rejects retroactive rule changes;
- updates the scoreboard;
- signals the end of the round.

The Lamp never supplies a solution, takes sides, or speaks dialogue. Its visual feedback makes complex logic readable without adding another talking character.

## 7. Tactical depth limit

Every episode may contain at most three meaningful strategic turns:

1. Genie's exploit.
2. Owner's counter-trap.
3. Optional Genie counter-counter.

More layers may sound intelligent on paper but become incomprehensible in a 50–75 second mobile video. Intelligence must be visible through cause and effect, not long exposition.

## 8. Outcome families

### A. Genie Win

The Owner blocks the obvious loophole but misses a deeper consequence.

Viewer feeling: "The Genie was still one step ahead."

### B. Owner Win

The apparent loophole was bait. The Genie's chosen fulfillment activates the Owner's preparation.

Viewer feeling: "The entire wish was a trap."

### C. Mutual Defeat

Both plans are logically sound, but their interaction creates a third consequence that hurts both characters.

Viewer feeling: "They outsmarted each other and still lost."

### D. Pyrrhic Victory

One character wins the round but pays a larger practical or emotional price than expected.

Viewer feeling: "He won — and somehow lost worse."

No public fixed rotation controls these outcomes. The winner must follow from the episode's declared rules and planted evidence, not an editorial quota.

## 9. Narrative structure — serialized animation, not HEIT

OUTWISHED is a serialized animated story, not an educational explainer. Episodes do not use Hook–Explain–Illustrate–Teach and do not force a `Teach` takeaway. They use dramatic causality, character conflict, planted visual evidence, and a closing story stinger.

Default episode range remains 50–75 seconds. A common shape is:

| Time | Dramatic beat | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | Cold open | Begin on a dangerous, surprising, or contradictory action; preserve an unresolved question. |
| 4–18s | Threat and immediate goal | Establish what Nico wants and what can be lost through action rather than exposition. |
| 18–32s | Complication or betrayal | Invalidate the obvious solution and intensify the character conflict. |
| 32–45s | Low point and discovery | Reveal the usable object, fact, or environmental clue. |
| 45–58s | Wording trap | Lock one understandable wish or verbal move. |
| 58–68s | Magical fulfillment and reversal | Recontextualize a clue that appeared before the wish. |
| 68–75s | Consequence and stinger | Resolve the episode's immediate problem while opening the next character problem. |

This is a flexible dramatic template, not a mandatory timestamp grid. Origin episodes may delay the first wish while introducing the world. No episode may replace causality with explanatory narration merely to fit a framework.

## 10. Character 1 — The Owner

Working name: **Nico Vale**  
Role archetype: the Planner  
Strategic style: preparation, prediction, second-order effects

### Personality

- observant, patient, and quietly competitive;
- learns from every prior loss;
- deliberately leaves attractive false loopholes;
- prefers making the opponent choose the losing move;
- enjoys the reveal almost as much as the reward;
- remains relatable through ordinary desires and practical frustrations.

### Strengths

- models what the Genie expects him to do;
- prepares the physical environment before speaking;
- remembers old rulings from the Lamp;
- identifies patterns in the Genie's taste for theatrical punishments;
- can sacrifice a small benefit to win the larger objective.

### Flaws

- cannot resist showing that he was clever;
- sometimes optimizes logic while misreading emotion;
- over-engineers simple problems;
- greed and pride can override an otherwise sound plan;
- assumes every variable can be controlled.

### Comedy behavior

- stays unnervingly calm while a visible disaster approaches;
- reveals absurdly specific preparations from pockets, walls, or furniture;
- packs for his reward before the wish is complete;
- when wrong, suppresses panic and pretends the failure was "within tolerance";
- uses a small loophole notebook that becomes increasingly overstuffed across episodes.

### Visual design

- young adult, lean compact build; grounded human proportions;
- warm, readable palette: mustard, coral, charcoal, and teal accents;
- cropped mustard utility jacket with one teal rolled sleeve and visible inner pockets;
- charcoal tapered trousers and red high-top shoes;
- dark swept hair with one question-mark-shaped forelock;
- pencil behind one ear; slim red loophole notebook clipped at the belt;
- asymmetrical clothing creates a recognizable silhouette without fantasy-prince coding;
- default expression: calm half-smile and one raised eyebrow;
- movement is economical and deliberate; he manipulates props rather than gesturing broadly.

### Expression set

1. **Calculated calm** — slight smile, raised eyebrow.
2. **Baiting innocence** — widened eyes that are visibly too innocent.
3. **Silent alarm** — fixed smile, tiny pupils, one sweat bead.
4. **Reveal satisfaction** — notebook closes with a decisive snap.
5. **Compressed defeat** — jaw tight, hair singed, still pretending to be composed.

### Voice direction

- conversational male voice, late 20s to early 30s;
- relaxed and precise rather than "genius" theatrical;
- accelerates only when a supposedly controlled plan collapses;
- humor comes from understatement.

### Character rule

Nico must never win because the Genie becomes stupid. He wins only because a preparation, sacrifice, or inference was established earlier.

## 11. Character 2 — The Genie

Working name: **Veyr**  
Role archetype: the Improviser  
Strategic style: literal interpretation, rapid reframing, theatrical misdirection

### Personality

- brilliant, playful, and dangerously curious;
- treats every wish as both a contract and a stage performance;
- enjoys elegant traps more than simple cruelty;
- trolls people through harmless, unnecessary presentation choices whenever the exact wish leaves him room;
- respects a good counter-plan even while furious about losing;
- constantly tests whether Nico has actually learned;
- hides genuine admiration beneath mockery.

### Strengths

- parses wording instantly;
- understands magical constraints better than Nico;
- notices unstated assumptions;
- adapts during fulfillment before execution becomes locked;
- weaponizes scale, timing, ownership, location, and collateral effects;
- can plant a false loophole to conceal a deeper one.

### Flaws

- addicted to applause and dramatic reveals;
- chooses the cleverest-looking implementation instead of the safest one;
- underestimates mundane physical details;
- hates predictable solutions and may reject an easy win;
- becomes visibly unstable when Nico refuses to react.

### Comedy behavior

- produces glowing diagrams no one requested;
- pauses for applause after saying one devastating word;
- uses miniature magical replicas to demonstrate how Nico will lose;
- rehearses victory poses too early;
- when defeated, his smoke body briefly collapses into an undignified household shape.

### Visual design

- original non-human smoke entity; not a muscular blue humanoid;
- slender floating body in deep violet and plum smoke with cyan edge-light;
- tapered question-mark smoke tail instead of legs;
- faceted golden eyes and a thin luminous grin;
- two detached floating forearms with long expressive fingers;
- asymmetrical smoke fins form a crescent collar and readable upper silhouette;
- small glowing rule tiles orbit his hands when parsing a wish;
- no turban, vest, harem trousers, branded lamp costume, or familiar copyrighted-genie silhouette;
- default expression: theatrical smirk with one angular brow;
- motion is expansive, elastic, and intentionally excessive.

### Expression set

1. **Mock innocence** — open palms, halo-like smoke ring.
2. **Loophole found** — golden eyes narrow into sharp diamonds.
3. **Premature triumph** — full grin, smoke fireworks.
4. **Counter-trap detected** — grin freezes while one smoke wisp retreats.
5. **Painful defeat** — smoke collapses into a dented kettle shape before reforming.

### Voice direction

- agile, warm, theatrical male voice with a smiling delivery;
- moves from intimate whisper to stage-announcer scale;
- never sounds generically evil;
- anger becomes overly polite rather than loud.

### Character rule

Veyr must never win through undefined omnipotence. Every win must use exact wording, a declared world rule, or a clue viewers could inspect.

## 12. Rival chemistry

Nico and Veyr are strategic opposites:

| Dimension | Nico | Veyr |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligence | Prepared | Improvised |
| Tempo | Patient | Explosive |
| Humor | Understatement | Theatrical exaggeration |
| Weapon | Environment | Wording |
| Blind spot | Human unpredictability | Mundane physical details |
| Desire | Control the outcome | Control the interpretation |
| Emotional flaw | Pride in planning | Pride in performance |

Their relationship should evolve from hostility into competitive respect without becoming friendly enough to remove stakes.

Recurring exchange pattern:

```text
Nico: says something suspiciously simple.
Veyr: assumes he found the weakness.
Nico: remains calm during apparent defeat.
Veyr: realizes the calm itself was a clue.
```

## 13. Season spine

### Phase 1 — First Contact

The origin may let Nico win through superior knowledge of his environment, establishing that he is a credible rival rather than a permanent victim. Veyr immediately recognizes the threat and begins testing Nico's wording in later rounds. No fixed winner rotation applies.

### Phase 2 — The Notebook

Nico records every ruling and begins blocking known loopholes.

### Phase 3 — The Bait Era

Nico intentionally leaves false weaknesses. Veyr starts analyzing preparation rather than wording alone.

### Phase 4 — False Loopholes

Veyr plants an obvious interpretation to manipulate Nico's counter-plan.

### Phase 5 — Golden Wish Duel

Both sides build multi-episode plans around the final seasonal prize.

Suggested stakes:

- if Nico wins the season, he receives one untwistable Golden Wish;
- if Veyr wins, he earns freedom from the Lamp;
- the public scoreboard records round wins but never dictates the next result.

```text

```

## 15. Community loop

Primary prompts:

- `Who planted the real trap — OWNER or GENIE?`
- `What clue did you spot?`
- `Rewrite the wish so both plans fail.`
- `Can you write a wish that beats both of them?`

A strong audience submission may become a later episode, but the production record must preserve the exact submitted wording and test it under the same published rules.

## 16. Quality gates

Reject an episode if any answer is `no`:

1. Can a new viewer understand the desire in under three seconds?
2. Is the exact wish readable and speakable in one short burst?
3. Is the decisive clue visible before the reveal?
4. Does the Genie's exploit obey Minimum Magic?
5. Does the Owner demonstrate learning rather than luck?
6. Does each escalation change the problem, not merely enlarge it?
7. Can either rival plausibly win until the final turn?
8. Is the outcome caused by logic rather than author intervention?
9. Does the loser receive an ironic visual consequence?
10. Does the callback reuse an established line, prop, or rule?
11. Can the story be understood with minimal language-specific wordplay?
12. Does the final cut remain within the project's 50–75 second range?

## 17. Known strategic risks

### Both characters become "smart" only through dialogue

Mitigation: reveal intelligence through props, positions, timing, and physical cause-and-effect. Keep explanations shorter than actions.

### The writer hides required information

Mitigation: enforce the Visible Clue rule and perform a rewind test after outlining.

### The Genie always wins

Mitigation: preserve all four outcome families and let rules determine the result.

### The Owner always has a retroactive gadget

Mitigation: every preparation must appear before `WISH LOCKED`; no new solution objects after the exploit.

### Counter-counter escalation becomes confusing

Mitigation: cap strategic turns at three and use the Lamp's visual validation state.

### Language puns limit global reach

Mitigation: prioritize quantity, timing, scale, ownership, location, and visual second-order effects over homophones or English-only ambiguity.

### The premise is broad but not yet validated

Mitigation: treat this bible as a concept hypothesis. A separate approved prototype and audience test are required before changing vertical topology, MAB state, production policy, or publishing cadence.

## 18. Initial visual hypothesis

- original 2D cut-out/sprite characters with bold outlines and flat cel shading;
- silhouettes remain distinguishable at mobile size;
- Nico uses a warm grounded palette; Veyr uses a cool luminous palette;
- every 0–5 second hook contains continuous movement and a visible face/reaction;
- magical diagrams support action but never replace it;
- episode environments remain ordinary and relatable so magical disruption reads instantly;
- the Lamp is a compact recurring rule interface, not a dialogue-heavy third mascot.

The initial character lineup is a style-feasibility artifact only. Character consistency, animation rigging, motion quality, and the final production format require separate validation.
