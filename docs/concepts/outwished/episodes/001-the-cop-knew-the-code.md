# OUTWISHED 001 — The Cop Knew the Code

## Episode purpose

This origin episode introduces Nico, Veyr, the modern Vale estate, and the hidden Lamp through a home-invasion thriller that turns into magical contract comedy.

It must establish that:

- Nico is a wealthy but active protagonist, not a helpless rich victim;
- his family fortune and mansion descended from Aladdin's bloodline, but the Lamp was sealed and forgotten rather than used to maintain the wealth;
- the uniformed officer is a real but corrupt officer working with the burglars;
- Nico discovers the Lamp only because the intruders lock him in a neglected basement room;
- Nico's first wish is simple enough to understand on first listen and catches all three criminals;
- Veyr fulfills the useful part exactly, then stacks the thieves into an unnecessarily ridiculous human tower;
- the thieves are defeated, while the final line reveals that Veyr enjoys trolling people for his own amusement.

## Narrative model

This episode does **not** use HEIT. It is a serialized animated-film episode with this dramatic progression:

```text
COLD OPEN
→ HOME INVASION
→ ESCAPE AND POLICE CALL
→ FALSE RESCUE
→ CORRUPT-OFFICER REVEAL
→ BASEMENT LOW POINT
→ LAMP DISCOVERY
→ FIRST WISH
→ VAULT LOCKDOWN
→ TROLL STINGER
```

There is no educational `Teach` beat and no forced explanatory takeaway. Character decisions, visual clues, suspense, and payoff carry the story.

## Refined premise

One month after inheriting the Vale family mansion, Nico is attacked by two burglars. He escapes long enough to call emergency services. A uniformed officer arrives and appears to save him, but Nico realizes the officer knows the private vault code. The officer is the burglars' third partner.

The corrupt officer has Nico locked in an old basement storage room while the trio empties the vault. Before the door closes, Nico hears one simple warning: if the vault closes with anyone inside, its safety alarm calls the police.

Searching the basement for another exit, Nico sees a green glint in its darkest corner. His inherited signet opens a bloodline-sealed wall compartment containing Aladdin's hidden Lamp. Veyr awakens.

Nico makes one direct wish: lock all three thieves inside the vault. Veyr includes the corrupt officer, teleports the trio into the vault, and closes the door. The safety alarm calls honest police.

Nico defeats the immediate threat. Veyr deliberately stacks the thieves into a ridiculous human tower even though the wish never required it, revealing that he treats other people's panic as entertainment.

## Character states

### Nico Vale

- late 20s/early 30s, direct descendant of Aladdin;
- financially comfortable through inherited family businesses and property, not current magic;
- recently inherited the mansion and has not explored every sealed room;
- observant under pressure: remembers the simple vault-alarm warning and turns it into a trap;
- dry humor remains intact even while cuffed and robbed.

### Veyr

- awakens only after the vault-alarm clue has already been spoken;
- recognizes Nico's bloodline immediately;
- grants the simple wish exactly by moving and locking the three thieves in one execution;
- chooses a ridiculous stacked arrangement during that same teleport because it amuses him and costs no additional fulfillment step;
- enjoys harmless humiliation and a reaction more than efficient dignity;
- ends the episode openly admitting that the useful result was Nico's request, but the troll was his own idea.

### Corrupt officer

- a genuine officer with access to police equipment, not a costume-only impersonator;
- used his position to take the dispatch before another unit was sent because he planned the burglary;
- turns off his body camera before revealing himself;
- knows the vault code and security system from prior reconnaissance;
- is legally and morally a thief at the moment of the wish, so the wording includes him.

## Exact wish

> **NICO:** "I wish all three thieves were locked inside the vault."

### Why the wording includes the officer

The wish targets conduct, not clothing, title, or mask. The officer is knowingly participating in the theft, demands a share, and is physically inside the house.

### Why the execution is simple

- the story has shown exactly three active thieves: two burglars and the corrupt officer;
- `locked inside the vault` requires only one group teleport and one closed door;
- the existing safety alarm, not a second magical action, calls honest police;
- Veyr chooses to stack them while positioning the group, revealing personality without adding a new wish outcome.

### Why Nico's plan works

- the officer explains the vault's safety alarm before the wish;
- Nico visibly hears the warning through the closing basement door;
- the safety sensor and police alert are shown before the Lamp is found;
- closing the vault is already part of fulfilling `locked inside`;
- no new security capability appears during the reveal.

## Detailed timeline

### 0.00–4.00 — Cold open: the wrong rescuer

**Visual and camera**

Frame zero is a direct vertical over-the-shoulder composition. Nico fills the left foreground in tense three-quarter profile. On the right, a uniformed police officer enters the private six-digit vault code without hesitation. Two masked burglars stand apart in the background with empty cash bags.

The camera pushes in slowly. Nico's eyes move from the keypad to the officer. After the final key press, the officer touches his body camera and its red light switches off.

**Dialogue**

> **NICO (V.O.):** "That officer knew my safe code. I'd never met him."

**Caption bursts**

- `0.20s` — `THAT OFFICER`
- `0.82s` — `KNEW MY SAFE CODE`
- `2.40s` — `I'D NEVER MET HIM`

**Sound**

- `0.00s`: metallic keypad `BEEP`;
- `0.40s`: body-camera power-down chirp;
- heartbeat enters under the last code digit;
- vault bolt begins moving, then hard-rewinds into the previous scene.

**Open question**

Why does the rescuer know the code, and how can Nico survive a police officer who controls the response?

---

### 4.00–9.00 — Ten minutes earlier: intrusion

**Visual and camera**

The camera races backward through the modern mansion: large but lived-in, with contemporary furniture, framed family-business photographs, and one abstract Aladdin-era trade-map motif—no palace or Disney imagery. Nico's slim red pocket notebook is visibly clipped inside his jacket.

A glass door sensor flashes red. Two burglars enter through the service wing. Nico spots their reflections in a dark television screen before they see him. He slides behind the kitchen island as one burglar crosses foreground.

**Dialogue**

> **NICO (V.O.):** "Ten minutes earlier, two men broke into my house."

**Sound**

- reversed vault bolt resolves into breaking glass;
- restrained alarm pulse;
- soft shoe movement and bag zipper.

---

### 9.00–15.00 — Escape and emergency call

**Visual and camera**

Nico throws a metal serving tray across the opposite hallway. Both burglars follow the noise. Nico opens a concealed service door behind a pantry shelf and slips into a narrow corridor.

While moving, he calls emergency services from his smartwatch. A dispatch waveform confirms the call. One burglar notices the closing shelf and gives chase, but Nico reaches the front hall and locks the service passage behind him.

**Dialogue**

> **NICO (low voice):** "Home invasion. Two suspects. Send police."

**Character note**

Nico does not overpower the burglars. He survives by reading reflections, redirecting attention, and knowing his environment.

---

### 15.00–20.00 — False rescue

**Visual and camera**

Police lights wash across the mansion windows. A single uniformed officer enters with professional confidence. He orders both burglars to the floor. One drops his tool; the other raises his hands.

Nico emerges. The officer positions himself between Nico and the burglars, apparently protecting him. His hand rests on Nico's shoulder a fraction too firmly.

**Dialogue**

> **OFFICER:** "You're safe now, sir."
>
> **NICO (exhaling):** "That was fast."

**Sound**

- distant siren cuts abruptly when the car stops;
- handcuff ratchet used on one burglar;
- musical tension appears to resolve, then one low note remains.

---

### 20.00–24.00 — Betrayal reveal

**Visual and camera**

The officer quietly cuffs Nico instead. He turns off his body camera, walks to the vault, and enters the private code. We arrive at the exact cold-open composition.

Nico looks from the officer to the supposedly restrained burglar. The burglar slips one hand effortlessly out of a deliberately loose cuff.

**Dialogue**

> **NICO:** "Why do you know my code?"
>
> **OFFICER (to the burglars):** "You took long enough."

**Sound**

- body-camera chirp repeats from the hook;
- loose cuff falls to the floor;
- vault bolts unlock.

---

### 24.00–31.00 — The fair-play clue

**Visual and camera**

The vault opens onto shelves of cash reserves, legal documents, and ancestral gold pieces. A small sensor above the door counts occupants. Its safety panel reads:

`SAFETY SENSOR: ARMED`

`PERSON INSIDE + DOOR CLOSED → POLICE ALERT`

The officer wedges the heavy door open while the burglars carry bags toward it. Nico is pushed toward the basement stairs but looks back when he hears the warning.

**Dialogue**

> **OFFICER:** "Keep the vault open. If it closes with anyone inside, the police get an alert."

**Visual hold requirement**

Hold the safety warning clearly for at least `1.5s`. Nico and the audience must understand one idea: closing the vault with someone inside calls the police. Do not hide it behind captions.

**Sound**

- safety-sensor chirp as one burglar reaches inside;
- one quiet warning tone when the officer briefly moves the door;
- basement door opens below.

---

### 31.00–36.00 — Basement low point

**Visual and camera**

The burglars remove Nico's phone and smartwatch but miss the red notebook already established inside his jacket. They lock him in a neglected basement storage room. The room contains old wine racks, sheet-covered furniture, a workbench, and one pre-established wheeled packing cart.

As the door closes, Nico can still hear bags being dragged toward the vault above. He tests the door, then scans the walls rather than wasting time kicking it.

**Dialogue**

> **OFFICER:** "Lock him downstairs."
>
> **NICO (to himself):** "This is not the rescue I ordered."

**Sound**

- basement lock turns;
- footsteps recede;
- vault loading sounds continue faintly through a floor vent.

---

### 36.00–44.00 — The Lamp in the darkest corner

**Visual and camera**

The power flickers. In the darkest corner, behind a collapsed cloth, Nico sees one small green glint. It comes from a geometric seal set into the wall—not from the Lamp itself.

Nico's inherited signet ring fits the seal. The wall opens a hand-width, revealing:

- an old transfer inscription naming the next Vale heir;
- Aladdin's red-marked family symbol;
- the brass puzzle-lamp, untouched and covered in dust.

The seal scans Nico's hand and displays:

`HEIR VERIFIED — NICO VALE`

When Nico lifts the Lamp, his thumb rubs dust across its green ring. Violet smoke snaps upward and resolves into Veyr, compressed by the low ceiling and visibly annoyed by the cramped room.

**Dialogue**

> **VEYR:** "Aladdin's heir. Your family took long enough."
>
> **NICO:** "Can you grant wishes?"
>
> **VEYR:** "I obey wording. Results vary."

**Ownership logic**

The transfer inscription and bloodline seal establish that the Lamp was specifically bequeathed to Nico, separate from the house contents being stolen. The family fortune survived through ordinary businesses; the sealed Lamp has not been generating it.

---

### 44.00–53.00 — Nico's first wish

**Visual and camera**

Through the floor vent, Nico confirms that the officer and both burglars are still beside the open vault. He opens the red notebook and writes two simple facts:

- `3 THIEVES`
- `CLOSED VAULT = POLICE ALERT`

There is no hidden legal puzzle. Nico needs Veyr to move the three criminals into one room and close its door.

The Lamp projects the exact sentence and waits.

**Dialogue — exact locked wording**

> **NICO:** "I wish all three thieves were locked inside the vault."
>
> **VEYR (glancing toward the officer):** "Even the one in uniform?"
>
> **NICO:** "Especially him."

The Lamp stamps:

`WISH LOCKED`

**Interpretation note**

The scene has already counted two burglars plus the corrupt officer. Veyr's clarification makes the inclusion explicit for the audience without adding a new condition after the wish.

---

### 53.00–61.00 — Wish granted, with unnecessary style

**Visual and camera**

Veyr snaps once. All three criminals vanish and reappear inside the vault in a ridiculous vertical stack:

1. the corrupt officer crouches at the bottom;
2. one burglar sits on his shoulders;
3. the second burglar balances on top, still clutching an empty cash bag.

The arrangement is safe but humiliating. It is simply the position Veyr chooses during the required group teleport. Before they can climb down, the heavy vault door closes and locks.

**Dialogue**

> **VEYR:** "Granted."
>
> **OFFICER:** "Why are we stacked?"

**Sound**

- three quick teleport pops rising in pitch;
- one comic wobble squeak as the human tower sways;
- heavy vault-door slam;
- Veyr suppresses a laugh.

---

### 61.00–67.00 — The simple trap closes

**Visual and camera**

The sensor above the door counts the people inside:

`OCCUPANTS: 3 — DOOR LOCKED`

The hardwired panel immediately sends:

`ALERT SENT — POLICE DISPATCH`

The stack collapses into a harmless pile behind the small reinforced window. The corrupt officer pounds on the glass as a new police unit accepts the alert.

**Dialogue**

> **OFFICER:** "Open this door!"
>
> **NICO:** "The other officers got the alert."

**Logic note**

The audience only needs to remember one planted rule: close the occupied vault and other police are notified.

---

### 67.00–74.00 — Veyr reveals the troll

**Visual and camera**

Honest police sirens grow louder outside. The criminals remain trapped and the immediate danger is over. Nico closes the red notebook and looks at Veyr, who is visibly pleased with himself rather than relieved.

On a small floating replay tile, Veyr loops the criminals' wobbling human tower one more time. He watches Nico's reaction with a thin golden grin.

**Dialogue**

> **NICO:** "Why did you stack them?"
>
> **VEYR:** "Locking them was your wish. The stack was my idea."

**End frame**

Veyr rewinds the wobble again and silently scores it with his fingers like an art judge: `9.5`. Nico realizes the ancient being he inherited is not solemn, obedient, or normal—he is a brilliant troll with unlimited patience. Red-blue light sweeps through the basement window. Cut to black on Veyr's delighted grin.

**Sound**

- approaching police-dispatch radio;
- replayed wobble squeak;
- Veyr's quiet, satisfied chuckle;
- playful unresolved OUTWISHED motif.

## Continuity and fairness audit

- The corrupt officer knows the code because he planned the robbery; Nico has never met him.
- The officer is a real officer, making the betrayal stronger and explaining his knowledge of the vault's police alert.
- The occupied-vault alarm appears before the Lamp and remains visible for `1.5s`.
- Veyr awakens after the clue, so he does not know that closing the occupied vault alerts other police.
- All three criminals are actively stealing when Nico speaks.
- The wish contains one simple fulfillment: move all three thieves into the vault and lock the door.
- The safety sensor and police alert are existing house systems, not new magical conditions.
- The human tower is the orientation Veyr chooses during the required group teleport, not a second wish effect.
- Nico solves the crisis through one remembered fact; Veyr uses the ending to reveal his taste for unnecessary trolling.
- The Lamp belongs to Nico through an explicit transfer inscription and bloodline verification, independently of the stolen house contents.
- The criminals are trapped awaiting arrest without gore or serious injury.
