## Observed grammar in the higher-view references

1. **Continuous encounter** — the opening remains in one real place with the same people. No abstract card or unrelated stock shot replaces the interaction.
2. **Stable protagonist** — every question and answer refers to the same identifiable person.
3. **Visible proof object** — Rolls-Royce, G-Wagon, or a recognized company/product gives the viewer something concrete to decode.
4. **Explicit question early** — the viewer does not have to infer what the video is investigating.
5. **Identity or mechanism reveal by roughly 8–16s** — gel nails, gravel-removal trucks, world's largest waterpark, or Venmo.
6. **Original voice and conversational causality** — host question and subject answer remain adjacent.
7. **Escalation through answers** — origin, number, hardship and lesson are asked in a sequence instead of stitched as unrelated declarations.
8. **Caption follows speech** — captions clarify the conversation; they do not introduce a competing editorial thesis.

## What the matched lower examples rule out

- `THIS` in the title is not sufficient: both `icox-xKoGKc` and `Y2dkRTTELz4` use nearly identical `inventing THIS` packaging but have very different view velocity.
- Clarity alone is not sufficient: Pictionary is identified by 8.4s, yet the hook has no visible product/status proof and the 120s edit later contains a long Shopify promotion.
- Being a millionaire is not a concrete premise: `yJtINrOAlbU` delays the company mechanism and mixes finance, horse riding and family-background threads.
- Duration is not the transferable driver: the references span 86–120s, while the project remains locked to 50–75s.

## Why V14A/V14B failed the clarity test

### V14A

- Opens on `250 jigsaws`, an implementation detail before the viewer knows the problem or product.
- Does not establish `car crash → pain → need → prototype` first.
- The semantic bridge from dismantled jigsaws to Theragun is not stated when needed.
- A later `saved my life` quote introduces a different emotional frame without preserving the patient chronology.
- The video therefore contains several interesting facts but no stable question.

### V14B

- Opens on `this thing`, whose referent is unknown.
- Jumps from customer outcome backward to prototype, then backward again to the crash.
- Theragun is named too late relative to the promise.
- The chronology forces viewers to reconstruct the causal chain themselves.

### Shared production error

The edits began with quote selection rather than premise selection. Full-screen illustration and editorial cards added information layers but could not repair the missing causal chain.

## Three candidate remake approaches

### A — Crash → First Theragun (recommended V15A)

Premise: **A motorcycle crash left a chiropractor with pain his own clinic could not fix, so he built the first Theragun.**

Story spine:

1. Identify Jason and the massage-gun invention.
2. Ask whether he expected a huge company.
3. Show the crash.
4. State the clinic had no solution.
5. Show the vibrating-table discovery.
6. Name the first Theragun.
7. Close on problem-born company / life-saving payoff.

Why recommended: the source itself supplies a chronological, self-contained 66.84s narrative with explicit referents and no invented bridge.

### B — Jigsaw in a Paper Bag → Product-Market Signal (recommended V15B)

Premise: **The first Theragun proved itself on one patient before athletes revealed the real market.**

Source plan:

1. `604.92–616.90` — a Makita jigsaw enters his chiropractic clinic; host clarifies his profession.
2. `617.04–624.34` — a patient arrives.
3. `671.66–682.10` — paper-bag jigsaw is used; visible reaction.
4. `787.54–800.60` — patient: `this thing saved my life... do something about this`.
5. `976.64–982.42` — one sale a day to chiropractors and therapists.
6. `987.10–1003.34` — athletes empty his trunk; he realizes the broader market.

Raw selected duration is approximately 64.5s before bounded finishing. The opening caption must explicitly name `THE FIRST THERAGUN` so `jigsaw` is an explanation, not an orphan curiosity hook.

Why recommended: it is a distinct product-validation story, not a second retelling of the crash.

### C — 250 Jigsaws → Garage Assembly (reject)

Premise: he bought 250 jigsaws and assembled primitive units from hardware-store parts.

Why rejected: this repeats the failed V14A attention object, requires more editorial bridging, and ends on manufacturing novelty rather than a human/product payoff.

## Creative rules for both remakes

- Use original source voice; no narrator bridge unless a missing causal link cannot be expressed with source audio.
- Preserve adjacent host question → subject answer wherever possible.
- One stable protagonist and one premise per video.
- Explicit product identity by 0–8s.
- No unresolved `this thing` before its object is visible/named.
- Proof footage must show the crash consequence, vibration mechanism, prototype/tool, patient or product-market signal; no decorative luxury/abstract stock.
- Do not place the mandatory CTA before the first complete payoff. If current ADR-0034 timing conflicts, seek a scoped ADR revision rather than interrupting causality.
- Run a cold-viewer clarity gate before final render: after one viewing, a reviewer must reproduce the premise without access to title/description.

## Recommended decision

Build A and B. Do not revise the rejected V14 renderer in place; keep it as a failed baseline and create a new V15 renderer/timeline so the causal change remains auditable.
