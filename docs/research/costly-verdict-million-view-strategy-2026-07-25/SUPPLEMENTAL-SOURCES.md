# Ronald Wayne Supplemental Source Ledger

**Snapshot:** 2026-07-25  
**Purpose:** additional factual and visual source research for a 50–75 second MONEY BLINDSPOT Short using TTS and MrBeast-style frequent visual changes.

## Editorial finding

The strongest defensible story is not “a fool lost hundreds of billions.” It is:

> Twelve days after signing Apple's partnership agreement, Ronald Wayne removed his name, accepting $800 for his stake because the partnership exposed him to downside that his much younger, asset-poor partners did not share. He says he does not regret leaving; he regrets selling his copy of the contract for $500.

This wording preserves the contradiction without pretending a modern 10% valuation is a clean counterfactual.

## Article and magazine sources

### 1. CNN — primary interview profile

- **Title:** “The gambling man who co-founded Apple and left for $800”
- **Date:** 2010-06-24
- **URL:** https://edition.cnn.com/2010/TECH/web/06/24/apple.forgotten.founder/index.html
- **Useful evidence:** Wayne's failed slot-machine business left debts; that experience informed his risk aversion. Direct quote: “You make a decision based on your understanding of the circumstances, and you live with it.”
- **Visual use:** headline/source card only; no long article screenshot.

### 2. CNBC Make It — concise financial reconstruction

- **Title:** “Why Ronald Wayne sold his 10% stake in Apple for $800”

- **URL:** https://www.cnbc.com/2018/08/02/why-ronald-wayne-sold-his-10-percent-stake-in-apple-for-800-dollars.html

## MrBeast-style strategy extracted for this production

This production does not invent a new retention system. It applies the proven MrBeast mechanics already reflected in the project's successful V16 revision:

1. **First frame:** human face plus impossible number contrast.
2. **Immediate promise:** reveal why the “obvious idiot” might have been rational.
3. **No reset:** every sentence changes the viewer's model of the decision.
4. **Frequent editing:** a hard cut, source change, camera reframe, animated proof or layout change at least every 1.5 seconds in the final timeline.
5. **Visible stakes:** house/car/bank assets disappear on-screen while debt moves toward Wayne.
6. **Escalation:** `$800` → personal liability → he believed Apple would succeed → identity cost → `$500 contract` → `$1.59M auction`.
7. **Payoff closes the opening:** leaving Apple is not declared smart or stupid; the viewer must judge whether $800 bought freedom.
8. **Comment game:** “Name the fact that changed your verdict.”

## Copyright / transformation plan

- TTS narration is the continuous editorial spine.
- Each borrowed source-video interval remains under 3 seconds in this Short; no source contributes remotely close to 50% of its duration.
- Value-add layers include multi-source mashup, factual citations, animated liability model, counter-argument and custom data visualization.
- Wikimedia assets retain source/license records in the production document.
- Article appearances are brief headline/source cards, not substitutes for the underlying reporting.
- Fair use is a legal doctrine, not an automatic guarantee; the edit minimizes exposure but cannot eliminate claim risk.
