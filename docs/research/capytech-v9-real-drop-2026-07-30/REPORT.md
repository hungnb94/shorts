# Capytech V9 — Real Drop Escalation Research

Date: 2026-07-30
Status: production research complete; public performance unproven

## Trigger

The user rejected `2026-07-30-capytech_v8_phone_drop.mp4` despite its technical verifier pass:

1. `1 cm` and `100 m` read almost the same.
2. ZapBara's emotional state barely changed and felt boring.
3. Destruction differences were too blurred to create an emotional payoff.
4. The layout was understandable, but not retention-worthy.

This is a creative-gate failure, not a codec/QC failure.

## V8 root cause

V8 changed labels and timings more strongly than it changed perception. All rounds retained nearly the same fixed tabletop stage, phone silhouette, camera function and mascot body language. Damage was mostly surface detail on a phone-sized object, while emotion relied on small eye/mouth changes. At mobile scale those differences collapse.

The old verifier answered “does each semantic role exist?” It did not answer “can an unbriefed viewer distinguish all stages with text hidden?” V9 adds that as a blocking gate.

## Strategy change

Do not build another animation-only phone-drop shell. Use real moving destruction footage as physical proof. Keep animation only for:

- factual height/round annotations;
- source citations;
- damage-state comparison;
- a recurring ZapBara reaction sticker with radically different full-body silhouettes.

The truthful promise changes from “the same phone at four heights” to “a 300-foot phone splits while a different 1,000-foot phone still works—why?” The clips use different phones, surfaces and sources; the edit must never imply a controlled comparison.

## Repeatable mechanisms extracted

### 1. One dominant physical question

Every source answers a single readable question: “Will it survive?” The final result resolves the setup directly. V8 had this question but weakened it with a repeated stage and subtle result states.

### 2. Environment proves scale

Height is communicated by objects shrinking, a deep stairwell, drone altitude, skyline/ground parallax and long fall time—not by a number card. The number becomes confirmation rather than the only evidence.

### 3. Damage needs silhouette-level change

The four outcomes must be distinguishable at 270×480 with text hidden:

1. mostly intact slab;
2. spiderweb/shattered back;
3. screen assembly visibly separated;
4. visibly powered display after the higher 1,000-foot drop.

The fourth result is deliberately not “more destroyed” than the third. Its survival is the payoff and must be visually proven by a powered display, not by narration alone.

### 4. Emotion needs embodied silhouette change

The branded mascot is retained, but each reaction changes posture, limbs, spatial behavior and prop treatment:

1. smug, arms crossed, stable stance;
2. sweat/wince, one paw guarding face;
3. full panic, both paws on head, body shake;
4. disbelief reversal, frozen jaw-drop followed by an off-balance backward stumble.

Small eyebrow or mouth swaps do not count.

### 5. Escalation must use multiple channels

Each round must change at least four of five channels:

- environment/scale cue;
- camera function;
- object silhouette/damage;
- physics/consequence;
- embodied reaction.

Particles, UI labels and louder SFX cannot substitute for these changes.

## V9 concept

Working title: **A 300FT Drop Did MORE Damage Than 1,000FT**

Narrative promise: four real drop tests escalate through obvious damage, then the 1,000-foot result breaks the expected pattern and explains why height alone is not enough.

Target timeline: 54–59 seconds.

- 0–3s: moving host face → 300-foot split result → 1,000-foot aerial → powered display; no freeze.
- 3–12s: waist height — ordinary accident, light damage, smug mascot.
- 12–24s: 10 stories — stairwell depth and shattered back, worried mascot.
- 24–36s: 300 ft — long fall and screen separation, panic mascot.
- 36–49s: 1,000 ft — aerial/ground proof and freefall; triple CTA lands during continued airborne action at 38–42s.
- 49–56s: powered-display reveal + source reaction audio + disbelief mascot; commentary explains that phones, surfaces and methods differ.
- 56–58s: `SURFACE > HEIGHT?` question/loop reset.

## Transformative Gate

The edit must pass all three project rules:

1. original commentary track;
2. at least two value-adds—multi-source mashup, source citations, scale annotations, damage comparison and counter-context are planned;
3. every retained source clip is under 15 seconds, and total retained footage from each source is well below 50% of that source.

## Blocking creative gates

1. Frame 0 contains a moving human face; no static card/freeze.
2. Final 0–5s visual change every 0.8–1.5s.
3. Before full rendering, four exact proposed result crops are equalized at 270×480, labels are hidden and order is randomized; machine review is recorded, and any unavailable human cold-viewer result is marked `UNVERIFIED` rather than fabricated.
4. Before full rendering, four ZapBara reaction silhouettes are shown at final pixel size with labels/context hidden and randomized order; ambiguity requires redesign rather than larger labels, particles or SFX.
5. Each round changes at least four escalation channels.
6. Round 4 does not rely on particles or a number card to appear stronger.
7. CTA starts between 38–42s and physical action continues underneath.
8. Exact-final MP4 passes full decode, ffprobe, ASR, black/freeze/silence/audio and dense contact-sheet review.

## Honest confidence boundary

The research supports a better causal design than V8. It cannot establish a 99% probability of millions of views. Virality remains `VIRALITY_UNPROVEN` until the public artifact is distributed and stable metrics are available after at least 48 hours.
