## Transferable Engine

Apply these mechanisms:

1. Packaging establishes one extreme, legible expectation.
2. The first five seconds use authority, visual proof and quantified or concrete
   stakes—not an introduction.
3. Every visual change adds information: a new causal step, proof, contrast or
   question.
4. Front-load production density; do not spread effort evenly.
5. Use emotional/audio contrast between problem, discovery and payoff.
6. Use a deliberate short music drop before the mechanism or payoff.
7. Couple SFX to visible actions, annotations, transitions and implied motion.
8. Preserve one open loop until the body supplies the mechanism.
9. End by paying the initial question, not by opening a new unresolved lesson.

Do not copy these surface features blindly:

- No unsupported exaggeration or invented figures.
- No full-screen stock blackout of the speaker during 0–10s.
- No forced hard cut every 1.5s when it damages sentence comprehension.
- Do not treat relevant stock as archival proof; label illustrative footage.
- Do not attribute the reference's current view count causally to its edit.

All three outputs must satisfy:

- 1080×1920, H.264/yuv420p, AAC stereo 48kHz, 30fps.
- Runtime 50–75s.
- Original source voice only; no TTS.
- Frame 0 and the complete 0–10s window keep a human face visible.
- Caption visible by 0.2s, 2–5 words per burst, one emphasized keyword.
- Hook visual-information change every approximately 0.8–1.5s.
- Body visual-information change every approximately 2–3s; a change may be a

- First-second SFX; source-synced captions; purpose-matched transition/action

## Variant A — Product Is Not a Company

### Premise

A personal invention became a product; repeated demand from a specific market
turned it into a company.

### Hook

Source-native paradox:

> I had a product. I didn't have a company.

Hook overlay: `A PRODUCT ≠ A COMPANY`, followed by `WHAT WAS MISSING?` while
Jason's face remains visible.

Rubric score: 52/60.

### Causal spine

1. Product-without-company paradox.
2. Crash and clinic failure establish the original need.
3. Vibrating-table motion reveals the mechanism.
4. Jason builds the first Theragun.
5. He buys 250 jigsaws and turns invention into repeatable units.
6. Clinicians buy one per day.
7. Athletes repeatedly empty his trunk.
8. Athlete pull reveals the market; external validation enables the next phase.

### Payoff

`A PRODUCT SOLVES A PROBLEM. A COMPANY FINDS REPEATABLE DEMAND.`

Target runtime: 62–70s.

## Audio Design

Each variant uses a three-state score generated or sourced without paid spend:

1. Problem: restrained low pulse with sparse texture.
2. Discovery: brighter rhythmic layer introduced on mechanism realization.
3. Payoff: confident lift, then duck under the final lesson.

A 120–220ms deliberate music drop precedes the decisive mechanism/payoff line.
Dialogue is never silenced. SFX events are tied to visible states: crash impact,
constraint card, vibration/back-and-forth annotation, prototype reveal,
jigsaw-count timeline, sale counter, empty-trunk market reveal and CTA actions.
The mix must still measure near -16 LUFS and remain dialogue-led.

## Visual Proof Strategy

- Hook: source face occupies at least 60% of the portrait canvas; evidence uses a
  side or corner panel.
- Crash footage is illustrative and labeled, never presented as Jason's actual
  accident.
- Use animated causal cards and source citations where no archival image exists.
- Prefer actual source statements over decorative stock.
- Product/company, temporary/lasting and clinician/athlete contrasts use split
  states that change only when the spoken causal state changes.
- Full-screen illustration is allowed after 10s only when it explains the
  current noun/mechanism and does not show a competing brand.

## Verification and Selection

No renderer unit tests will be added. Each real MP4 must pass:

1. ffprobe/spec validation and full decode.
2. Hook, arc, evidence and CTA contact-sheet review.
3. Final ASR for hook, mechanism, CTA and payoff.
4. Black, freeze, silence and loudness checks.
5. Frame-0 and 0–10s face visibility.
6. Caption safe-zone and source/watermark/CTA collision checks.
7. Cold-viewer retell: who, problem, action/mechanism, proof and payoff.
8. Side-by-side creative adjudication across all three variants.

The three outputs are creative alternatives, not a controlled A/B test because
their hooks and bodies differ. User subjective selection chooses the production
candidate; future platform metrics evaluate it only after upload and the 48h
wait.
